From business requirements to T-SQL scripts

One store database and five tasks for different departments. Identify the rules in each client brief, choose the constructs covered in lecture 3, and write a working block of code.

Variables and conditions · working tables · loops · cursors · error handling

Shared database

Office equipment store

The store takes orders, manages stock, and reminds customers about payments. A customer may have no orders yet, and a product may never have been sold. Order items are numbered separately within each order.

Understanding the data

  • ListPrice is the current price, while UnitPrice in an order item is the price agreed when the order was placed. The order value is the sum of quantity multiplied by price for all its items. All amounts are in PLN; ignore VAT.
  • The same product may appear in several items within one order, including at different prices. Do not assume that an order–product pair is unique.
  • The statuses are New, Paid, Shipped, and Cancelled. “New” means that the order is awaiting payment. IsActive = 0 identifies an inactive customer or a product withdrawn from sale; its history remains in the database.
  • StockQuantity is the number of units currently available. Placing or paying for an order does not reserve stock. Stock decreases only when an order is shipped in exercise 5.
  • The database allows orders with no items. The empty paid order 1013 is a deliberately incomplete case that your code must detect. Other test cases include an inactive customer, a missing email address, an inactive product, and insufficient stock.
  • Work on your own copy of the database, in a single session, with no concurrent changes by other users. Start each test with no transaction open.

Exercise 1 · Customer service

Order pricing

Use in this exercise

DECLARESET / SELECT (assignment)DECIMALIF…ELSEBEGIN…ENDPRINTRETURN

Input: @OrderID INT.

Client brief

Before paying for an order, the customer wants to know the amount due. An employee enters the order number. If the order does not exist, the customer is inactive, the order is not new, or it has no items, display a clear message explaining why pricing cannot proceed, then stop.

We offer a 10% discount when the goods value is at least PLN 1000. For values from PLN 500 up to, but not including, PLN 1000, the discount is 5%. Below PLN 500, there is no discount. Check the thresholds before applying the discount. Round the discount amount to two decimal places and subtract it from the goods value.

Delivery is free when the value after discount is at least PLN 500. Otherwise, delivery costs PLN 20. Display the order number, goods value, discount percentage and amount, value after discount, delivery charge, and amount due. This is only a calculation: do not save it in the database or check product stock at this stage.

Hint: steps
  1. Read the order number.
  2. If the order does not exist, display a message and stop.
  3. If the customer is inactive, the order is not new, or it has no items, display the reason and stop.
  4. Calculate the goods value: the sum of quantity × price over all items.
  5. Choose the discount percentage from the thresholds (check the highest first) and calculate the discount amount.
  6. Value after discount = goods value − discount.
  7. Set the delivery charge: free from PLN 500 after discount, otherwise PLN 20.
  8. Amount due = value after discount + delivery. Display all the results.

Exercise 3 · Purchasing department

Restocking within a budget

Use in this exercise

DECLAREWHILEBREAKCONTINUEIF…ELSEBEGIN…ENDtable variable @PurchasePlanINSERTSELECT

Input and rules: @Budget DECIMAL(12,2) and @ProductLimit INT. The budget may be 0 but cannot be negative; the limit must be positive. Reject NULL as well. Every loop iteration must make progress or terminate.

Client brief

We have a limited budget for restocking and a limit on the number of different products we can order today. Prepare a purchase plan by considering products in ascending order of product number. Product numbers do not have to be consecutive.

Skip products that are inactive or already have stock at or above the minimum. For the remaining products, we want to buy exactly enough units to reach the minimum stock level. For this simulation, the purchase cost per unit equals the list price.

If the full restocking quantity for a product fits within the remaining budget, add it to the plan and reduce the available budget. If it is too expensive, skip that product and check the next one, which may be cheaper. Do not buy only part of the missing quantity. Stop when the budget is exhausted, the limit on different products is reached, or the entire catalogue has been considered.

Show the plan: product number and name, quantity, unit price, and line cost, ordered by product number. Separately display the total plan cost and remaining budget. An empty plan has a cost of 0. Do not change stock quantities: the decision to purchase has not been made yet.

Hint: steps
  1. Check the input. If it is invalid, display a message and stop.
  2. Prepare an empty plan, the remaining budget (= budget), and a counter of products in the plan (= 0).
  3. Go through the products in ascending order of product number and decide about each one (the rules are described below).
  4. Display the plan ordered by product number.
  5. Display the total cost and the remaining budget.

How the plan is built

Go through the products one by one, in ascending order of product number. For each product:

  1. Check the stop conditions first. Stop if no budget is left or the plan already contains the allowed number of different products (@ProductLimit).
  2. Skip the product if it is inactive or its stock is already at or above the minimum.
  3. Calculate the purchase: quantity = MinimumStock − StockQuantity; line cost = quantity × ListPrice.
  4. Decide: if the line cost is less than or equal to the remaining budget, add the product to the plan and subtract the cost from the budget. Otherwise skip it and move on: a later, cheaper product may still fit.

Every product is considered once, and a quantity is never reduced to fit the budget: a product is bought in full or not at all.

Test cases

  • Budget PLN 1000, limit 2: product 205 — 3 units costing PLN 240; product 420 — 2 units costing PLN 500. Total PLN 740, leaving PLN 260. Monitor 310 is too expensive, but it must not prevent the purchase of product 420.
  • Budget PLN 1000, limit 1: product 205 only, leaving PLN 760.
  • Budget PLN 240, limit 2: product 205 uses the entire budget exactly. A budget of PLN 200 or PLN 0 produces an empty plan.
  • Budget PLN 2000, limit 10: products 205, 310, and 420; total cost PLN 1640, leaving PLN 360. The loop stops even though the product limit has not been reached.

Exercise 4 · Accounts receivable

Overdue payment reminders

Use in this exercise

DECLARECURSOR LOCAL FAST_FORWARDOPENFETCH NEXT@@FETCH_STATUSWHILECLOSEDEALLOCATEIF…ELSEPRINTTRY…CATCH

Input and rules: @AnalysisDate DATE; if it is NULL, stop the block with a message. Release resources after a caught error as well.

Client brief

Our notification module accepts reminders one at a time. For a specified analysis date, process new, non-empty orders from active customers whose payment due date is earlier than that date. An order due on the analysis date itself is not overdue yet.

Process each order separately, in ascending order of order number. Each reminder must include the order number, email address, goods value using the order item prices, and number of days overdue. Do not use the pricing calculation from exercise 1. In this exercise, display the message text instead of sending it.

If the email address is unknown, skip the reminder, display the skipped order number and the reason, and continue. At the end, show the number of reminders prepared and the number of orders skipped because of a missing email address. Do not modify the store data.

Hint: steps
  1. Check the analysis date. If it is missing, display a message and stop.
  2. Set both counters (reminders prepared, orders skipped) to 0.
  3. Prepare the list of overdue orders: new, non-empty, active customer, due date before the analysis date, ordered by order number.
  4. Take the orders one at a time. If the email address is missing, display the order number and the reason and increase the skipped counter. Otherwise display the reminder text and increase the prepared counter.
  5. Release the cursor at the end, and also when an error occurs.
  6. Display both counters.

Comparison: also prepare a set-based SELECT that returns the same list of valid reminders without printing them individually. In a comment, explain which version you would choose if the module accepted the entire list at once.

Test cases

  • For 2026-10-15: order 1001 — PLN 320.00 and 10 days overdue; order 1002 — PLN 1150.00 and 9 days overdue. Two reminders.
  • Order 1008: missing email address; the skipped counter is 1. Order 1005 is excluded because the customer is inactive and does not increase this counter.
  • For 2026-10-05: no reminders, and both counters are 0. Order 1001 is not overdue yet.
  • Running the block again must not cause an error about an existing or open cursor. The set-based version returns the same orders, amounts, and days overdue.

Exercise 5 · Warehouse

Shipping an order without partial updates

Use in this exercise

SET XACT_ABORT ONTRY…CATCHBEGIN / COMMIT / ROLLBACK TRANSACTIONXACT_STATE()THROWERROR_NUMBER()ERROR_MESSAGE()UPDATE

Input and rules: @OrderID INT. A non-existent number or NULL is an error. Assign each reason for rejection its own error number of at least 50000.

Client brief

A warehouse employee enters the number of an order to ship. The order must exist, have the status “Paid”, and contain at least one item. All products must be active, and the warehouse must have enough units of each product.

The same product may appear in several order items. Check and deduct the total quantity of that product across the entire order. If even one product cannot be supplied, reject the whole operation and explain why.

On successful shipment, reduce the stock quantities of the relevant products and change the order status to “Shipped”. Both changes must be committed together. An error must leave neither partially reduced stock nor a changed status. A repeated attempt for an order that has already shipped must be rejected without deducting stock again.

On success, show the order number, new status, and number of units shipped. On error, display its number and message, roll back the changes, and rethrow the error to the program running the script.

Hint: steps
  1. Start a transaction.
  2. If the order does not exist, raise an error.
  3. If the status is not “Paid”, raise an error.
  4. If the order has no items, raise an error.
  5. Calculate the total quantity of each product in the order.
  6. If any product is inactive, raise an error.
  7. If any product has less stock than its total quantity, raise an error.
  8. Reduce the stock of each product and change the order status to “Shipped”.
  9. Commit the transaction and display the order number, new status, and number of units shipped.
  10. On any error: display its number and message, roll back, and rethrow it.

Test cases

  • 1003: ship 5 units in total. Stock for product 101 falls from 8 to 5, and stock for product 205 from 3 to 1. The status changes to Shipped. A repeated attempt is rejected with no further changes.
  • 1004: product 420 is out of stock. Stock for product 101 remains at 8, and the order status remains Paid.
  • 1014: reject. The two items require 9 keyboards in total, but only 8 are available. Checking each item separately would incorrectly allow the shipment.
  • 1012: inactive product; 1013: no items; 1001: incorrect status; 9999 and NULL: order not found. None of these attempts changes the data.
  • Rollback test: restore the database and, for order 1003, temporarily add a deliberate THROW after reducing stock but before changing the status. After the error, stock quantities must still be 8 and 3, and the status must be Paid. Remove this statement after the test.
  • After both a successful operation and a handled error, check in a separate batch that @@TRANCOUNT is 0.
